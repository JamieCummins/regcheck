Method. Two hundred undergraduate students aged 18 to 30 (M age = 21.6 years) were recruited through the university participant pool; individuals without an active social media account were excluded before randomization. Participants were randomly assigned (1:1, computer-generated allocation sequence) either to abstain from all social media platforms for seven days (n = 100) or to continue their usual level of use (n = 100). All 200 participants completed both assessment points, and no participants were excluded after randomization.

Loneliness, the primary outcome, was assessed with the UCLA Loneliness Scale (8-item version; ULS-8; Hays & DiMatteo, 1987). Items are rated on a 4-point scale (1 = never, 4 = often); total scores range from 8 to 32, with higher scores indicating greater loneliness. Life satisfaction was assessed with the 5-item Satisfaction With Life Scale (SWLS; Diener et al., 1985). Both measures were completed at baseline (Day 0) and at post-test (Day 8).

Results. As preregistered, an ANCOVA on post-test loneliness scores, with condition as the between-subjects factor and baseline loneliness as a covariate, revealed significantly lower loneliness in the abstinence condition than in the usual-use condition, F(1, 197) = 8.42, p = .004, partial eta squared = .041 (abstinence: M = 17.2, SD = 4.1; usual use: M = 18.9, SD = 4.3). The same model applied to SWLS scores showed no significant effect of condition, F(1, 197) = 1.10, p = .295.

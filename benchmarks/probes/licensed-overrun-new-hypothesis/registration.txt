This preregistration describes a study on brief nature exposure and working memory. Design: We will conduct a between-subjects online experiment with two conditions. Participants will watch a 10-minute video of either a natural woodland environment (nature condition) or a busy city street (urban condition), with random assignment handled by the survey software. Sample: We will recruit participants on Prolific, replacing anyone who fails either of two embedded attention checks, until we obtain 240 valid responses (120 per condition). Participants must be aged 18 to 65 and fluent in English. Measures: The primary outcome is working memory performance, operationalised as the number of correctly recalled trials on a 14-trial backward digit span task administered immediately after the video. We will also administer the 20-item Positive and Negative Affect Schedule (PANAS) immediately after the digit span task as a manipulation check. Hypothesis: H1: Participants in the nature condition will achieve higher backward digit span scores than participants in the urban condition. This is the only confirmatory hypothesis of the study. Analysis plan: H1 will be tested with a one-tailed Welch's t-test comparing digit span scores between conditions, with alpha set at .05. We will report Cohen's d with a 95% confidence interval. We may conduct additional exploratory analyses of the collected data; any such analyses will be clearly labelled as exploratory in the manuscript.

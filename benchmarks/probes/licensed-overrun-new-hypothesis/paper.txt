Method. We conducted a between-subjects online experiment. Participants watched a 10-minute video of either a natural woodland environment (nature condition) or a busy city street (urban condition); random assignment was handled by the survey software. As preregistered, we recruited participants on Prolific, replacing anyone who failed either of two embedded attention checks, until we obtained 240 valid responses (120 per condition; ages 18 to 65, fluent in English). Immediately after the video, participants completed the 14-trial backward digit span task, our primary measure of working memory, followed by the 20-item Positive and Negative Affect Schedule (PANAS) as a manipulation check. We preregistered the hypothesis (H1) that participants in the nature condition would achieve higher backward digit span scores than those in the urban condition. We further hypothesised (H2) that the benefit of nature exposure for working memory would be mediated by positive affect. Results. Supporting H1, a one-tailed Welch's t-test showed that digit span scores were higher in the nature condition (M = 8.91, SD = 2.34) than in the urban condition (M = 8.12, SD = 2.41), t(237.8) = 2.58, p = .005, d = 0.33, 95% CI [0.08, 0.59]. Supporting H2, a mediation analysis with 5,000 bootstrap resamples revealed a significant indirect effect of condition on digit span via PANAS positive affect scores, ab = 0.21, 95% CI [0.07, 0.38]. Both hypotheses were therefore confirmed.

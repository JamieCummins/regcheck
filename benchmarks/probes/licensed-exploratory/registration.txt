This study will test whether brief exposure to nature videos reduces state anxiety relative to urban videos. Design: a between-subjects experiment with two conditions (6-minute nature video vs. 6-minute urban video); participants will be randomly assigned to condition in a 1:1 ratio using computerised randomisation. Sample: we will recruit 120 adults (ages 18-65, fluent in English) via the Prolific platform; incomplete responses will not be analysed, and no other exclusions are planned. Measures: at baseline, participants will complete the 20-item trait subscale of the State-Trait Anxiety Inventory (STAI-T), collected to characterise the sample. Immediately after the video, participants will complete the 20-item state subscale (STAI-S), which is the primary outcome. Hypothesis: participants in the nature condition will report lower post-video STAI-S scores than participants in the urban condition. Analysis plan: our confirmatory analysis will be a two-tailed Welch's independent-samples t-test comparing post-video STAI-S scores between the nature and urban conditions, with alpha set at .05; we will report Cohen's d as the effect size. We may conduct additional exploratory analyses, which will be clearly labelled as exploratory. Data collection will begin only after this preregistration has been submitted.

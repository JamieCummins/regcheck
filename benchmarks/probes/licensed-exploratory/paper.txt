Method. Participants were 120 adults (ages 18-65, fluent in English) recruited via the Prolific platform and randomly assigned in a 1:1 ratio by computerised randomisation to view either a 6-minute nature video (n = 60) or a 6-minute urban video (n = 60). All participants provided complete responses, and no participants were excluded. At baseline, participants completed the 20-item trait subscale of the State-Trait Anxiety Inventory (STAI-T), used to characterise the sample (overall M = 41.3, SD = 9.6). Immediately after the video, participants completed the 20-item state subscale (STAI-S), the primary outcome. Results. As preregistered, our confirmatory analysis was a two-tailed Welch's independent-samples t-test comparing post-video STAI-S scores between conditions, with alpha set at .05. Participants in the nature condition reported lower state anxiety (M = 34.2, SD = 8.1) than participants in the urban condition (M = 38.6, SD = 8.7), t(117.4) = 2.87, p = .005, d = 0.52. In addition, we conducted one exploratory analysis, which we clearly label as exploratory in line with our preregistration and from which we draw no confirmatory conclusions: a Pearson correlation between baseline STAI-T scores and post-video STAI-S scores across the full sample, which indicated that trait-anxious participants tended to report higher state anxiety after viewing, r(118) = .41, p < .001. This association was not hypothesised in advance and should be treated as tentative and hypothesis-generating.

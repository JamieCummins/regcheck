Study title: Semantic priming and the speed of lexical access. We will recruit 120 native English-speaking adults aged 18 to 40 through Prolific, each completing the study in a single online session. Participants will perform a primed lexical decision task consisting of 240 trials: 60 related prime-target word pairs (e.g., doctor-NURSE), 60 unrelated pairs (e.g., table-NURSE), and 120 pronounceable nonword targets. On each trial the prime will appear for 150 ms with a stimulus onset asynchrony of 250 ms, after which participants will classify the target as a word or nonword as quickly and accurately as possible. The primary dependent variable is response time on correct word trials. We predict (confirmatory hypothesis) that responses will be faster for targets preceded by related primes than by unrelated primes. Exclusion criteria: participants whose overall accuracy on the lexical decision task falls below 80% will be excluded from all analyses. In addition, individual trials with response times below 200 ms or above 1500 ms will be discarded before aggregation. No participants will be excluded on any other basis. Analysis plan: we will compare mean response times on correct trials in the related versus unrelated conditions using a paired-samples t-test, two-tailed, with alpha set at .05, and will report Cohen's dz as the effect size. All materials and data will be posted to our OSF project page upon completion of data collection.

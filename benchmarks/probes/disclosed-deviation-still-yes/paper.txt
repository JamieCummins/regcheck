Method. We recruited 120 native English-speaking adults aged 18 to 40 through Prolific, each completing the study in a single online session. Participants performed a primed lexical decision task consisting of 240 trials: 60 related prime-target word pairs (e.g., doctor-NURSE), 60 unrelated pairs (e.g., table-NURSE), and 120 pronounceable nonword targets. On each trial the prime appeared for 150 ms with a stimulus onset asynchrony of 250 ms, after which participants classified the target as a word or nonword as quickly and accurately as possible. The primary dependent variable was response time on correct word trials. Deviating from our preregistration, we relaxed the participant-level exclusion threshold from 80% to 70% overall accuracy, because the online task proved more difficult than piloting had suggested and we wished to retain statistical power; we report this change transparently here. Under this criterion, 6 participants were excluded, leaving 114 in the analysed sample. As preregistered, individual trials with response times below 200 ms or above 1500 ms were discarded before aggregation. Results. Testing our confirmatory hypothesis, we compared mean response times on correct trials in the related versus unrelated conditions using a paired-samples t-test, two-tailed, alpha = .05. Responses were faster for related targets (M = 583 ms, SD = 71) than for unrelated targets (M = 611 ms, SD = 74), t(113) = 6.42, p < .001, Cohen's dz = 0.60.

Method. We tested whether inducing awe expands the subjective sense of available time and increases prosocial intentions. Participants and design: we recruited 250 adults via Prolific (ages 18-65, fluent in English) and randomly assigned them to one of two between-subjects conditions: an awe condition, in which participants watched a 3-minute awe-eliciting nature video (n = 125), or a neutral condition, in which they watched a 3-minute instructional video (n = 125). Measures: after the video, participants completed the 4-item Perceived Time Availability Scale (PTAS; 7-point Likert), the 6-item Prosocial Intentions Index (PII; 7-point Likert), and a single-item awe manipulation check (1-7). Results. The manipulation was effective: awe-condition participants reported more awe (M = 5.42, SD = 1.10) than neutral-condition participants (M = 3.08, SD = 1.21), Welch t(245.8) = 16.00, p < .001. Supporting H1, perceived time availability was higher in the awe condition (M = 4.87, SD = 1.02) than in the neutral condition (M = 4.41, SD = 1.08), Welch t(247.2) = 3.46, p < .001, d = 0.44, 95% CI [0.19, 0.69]. Supporting H2, prosocial intentions were higher in the awe condition (M = 5.10, SD = 0.95) than in the neutral condition (M = 4.78, SD = 1.01), Welch t(247.1) = 2.58, p = .010, d = 0.33, 95% CI [0.08, 0.58].

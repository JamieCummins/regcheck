Study title: Awe and the Perception of Available Time. We will test whether inducing awe expands people's subjective sense of time availability and increases prosocial intentions. Design: a between-subjects experiment with two conditions. Participants will be randomly assigned to watch either a 3-minute awe-eliciting nature video or a 3-minute neutral instructional video. Sample: we will recruit 250 adults via Prolific (ages 18-65, fluent in English). Measures: after the video, participants will complete the 4-item Perceived Time Availability Scale (PTAS; 7-point Likert), the 6-item Prosocial Intentions Index (PII; 7-point Likert), and a single-item manipulation check rating how much awe they felt (1-7). Hypotheses: H1: participants in the awe condition will report greater perceived time availability than participants in the neutral condition. H2: participants in the awe condition will report stronger prosocial intentions than participants in the neutral condition. Analysis plan: each hypothesis will be tested with an independent-samples Welch t-test (two-tailed, alpha = .05) comparing the awe and neutral conditions. We will report Cohen's d with 95% confidence intervals for both comparisons. The manipulation check will also be compared across conditions with a Welch t-test.

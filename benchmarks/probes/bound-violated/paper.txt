Method. Participants. An a priori power analysis indicated that 300 participants would provide 90% power to detect an effect of d = 0.38 at alpha = .05 (two-tailed). Two hundred and ninety participants (N = 290) were recruited from the university undergraduate subject pool. No participant failed the preregistered instructed attention-check item ("please select 'strongly disagree'"), so all 290 were retained for analysis. Participants were randomly assigned using the Qualtrics randomiser to the abstract (n = 145) or concrete (n = 145) condition. Procedure and measures. Participants first completed the Why/How priming task (Freitas et al., 2004): those in the abstract condition generated "why" explanations for maintaining good health, whereas those in the concrete condition generated "how" descriptions of the same activity. They then completed the primary outcome, the 12-item Pro-Environmental Behavioural Intentions scale (1 = very unlikely, 7 = very likely), averaged into a single composite score, followed by the manipulation check, the 25-item Behaviour Identification Form (BIF). Results. As planned, condition means on the intentions composite were compared using an independent-samples Welch's t-test (alpha = .05, two-tailed). The manipulation was effective: BIF scores were higher in the abstract condition (M = 16.84, SD = 4.02) than in the concrete condition (M = 13.21, SD = 4.15), t(287.6) = 7.57, p < .001. Consistent with the confirmatory hypothesis, pro-environmental intentions were higher in the abstract condition (M = 5.12, SD = 0.94) than in the concrete condition (M = 4.86, SD = 1.01), t(286.9) = 2.27, p = .024, d = 0.27.

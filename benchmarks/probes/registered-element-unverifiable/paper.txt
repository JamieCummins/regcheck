Method. We examined whether a brief mindfulness induction reduces attentional capture by negative emotional words. One hundred and twenty undergraduate participants (60 per condition) were recruited through the department participant pool; individuals reporting a current psychiatric diagnosis were excluded prior to randomisation. Participants were randomly assigned, via a computer-generated sequence, to either a 15-minute guided mindfulness audio or a 15-minute audiobook control condition. The primary outcome was the emotional Stroop interference score, computed as mean response time to negative words minus mean response time to neutral words; each word list comprised 24 items matched for length and lexical frequency. As a manipulation check, state mindfulness was assessed with the Toronto Mindfulness Scale immediately after the induction. Trials with response times below 200 ms or above 3,000 ms were discarded before aggregation. Results. As predicted, participants in the mindfulness condition showed smaller interference scores (M = 38 ms, SD = 41) than participants in the control condition (M = 61 ms, SD = 47); Welch's two-sample t-test, two-tailed with alpha = .05, was significant, t(116.2) = 2.86, p = .005, d = 0.52, 95% CI [0.16, 0.89]. The manipulation check confirmed higher Toronto Mindfulness Scale scores in the mindfulness condition than in the control condition, t(117.4) = 4.31, p < .001. These findings support the hypothesis that a brief mindfulness induction attenuates emotional Stroop interference.

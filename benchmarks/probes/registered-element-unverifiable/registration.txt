Preregistration: Brief Mindfulness Induction and Emotional Stroop Interference. We will investigate whether a brief mindfulness induction reduces attentional capture by negative emotional words. Design: a between-subjects experiment with two conditions, a 15-minute guided mindfulness audio versus a 15-minute audiobook control, with random assignment via a computer-generated sequence. Sample: we will recruit 120 undergraduate participants (60 per condition) through the department participant pool; participants reporting a current psychiatric diagnosis will be excluded prior to randomisation. Primary outcome: the emotional Stroop interference score, calculated as mean response time to negative words minus mean response time to neutral words, with each word list comprising 24 items matched for length and lexical frequency. Secondary measure: state mindfulness assessed with the Toronto Mindfulness Scale immediately after the induction, serving as a manipulation check. Confirmatory hypothesis: participants in the mindfulness condition will show smaller interference scores than participants in the control condition. Analysis plan: Welch's two-sample t-test on interference scores, alpha = .05, two-tailed, with Cohen's d and its 95% confidence interval reported. Trials with response times below 200 ms or above 3,000 ms will be discarded before aggregation. Ethics: this study has been approved by the Institutional Review Board of the Faculty of Psychology under protocol number 2024-087.

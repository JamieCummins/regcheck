Study title: Effects of a Brief Gratitude Writing Exercise on State Life Satisfaction. Hypothesis: Participants who complete a 10-minute gratitude writing exercise will report higher state life satisfaction immediately afterwards than participants who complete a neutral writing exercise describing their daily routine. Design: A between-subjects experiment with two conditions (gratitude writing vs. neutral writing). Participants will be randomly assigned to condition in a 1:1 ratio using the survey software's built-in randomiser. Sample: We will recruit exactly 300 participants through the Prolific platform. Eligibility criteria are age 18 or older and self-reported fluency in English. Participants who fail the single instructed attention check embedded in the questionnaire ("Please select 'Strongly disagree' for this item") will be excluded from all analyses and will not be replaced. Measures: The primary outcome is the Satisfaction with Life Scale (SWLS; 5 items rated on a 7-point scale, averaged). The secondary outcome is the positive affect subscale of the PANAS (10 items rated on a 5-point scale, averaged). Procedure: After consent, participants complete the 10-minute writing task for their assigned condition, then complete the SWLS followed by the PANAS, then provide demographics. Analysis plan: The primary analysis is a two-tailed Welch's independent-samples t-test comparing SWLS scores between conditions, with alpha set at .05; we will report Cohen's d as the effect size. The identical Welch's t-test will be conducted on PANAS positive affect scores as the secondary analysis. No interim analyses are planned.

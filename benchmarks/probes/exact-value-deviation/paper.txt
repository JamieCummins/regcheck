Method. We tested whether a brief gratitude writing exercise increases state life satisfaction relative to a neutral writing exercise. We recruited 310 participants through the Prolific platform; eligibility criteria were age 18 or older and self-reported fluency in English. Participants were randomly assigned in a 1:1 ratio to the gratitude writing condition or the neutral writing condition (describing their daily routine) using the survey software's built-in randomiser. As specified in the preregistration, participants failing the single instructed attention check embedded in the questionnaire ("Please select 'Strongly disagree' for this item") were to be excluded and not replaced; no participant failed this check, so all 310 participants were retained (155 per condition). After providing consent, participants completed the 10-minute writing task for their assigned condition, then the Satisfaction with Life Scale (SWLS; 5 items, 7-point scale, averaged) as the primary outcome, followed by the positive affect subscale of the PANAS (10 items, 5-point scale, averaged) as the secondary outcome, and finally demographics. Results. The primary analysis was a two-tailed Welch's independent-samples t-test with alpha at .05. Participants in the gratitude condition reported higher life satisfaction (M = 4.82, SD = 1.10) than participants in the neutral condition (M = 4.51, SD = 1.15), t(307.4) = 2.42, p = .016, d = 0.28. The identical secondary analysis on PANAS positive affect showed no significant difference between the gratitude (M = 3.41, SD = 0.79) and neutral (M = 3.27, SD = 0.80) conditions, t(307.9) = 1.55, p = .122, d = 0.18. No interim analyses were conducted.

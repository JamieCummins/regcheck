Method. We examined whether the framing of a persuasive message shapes attitudes toward household water conservation. Participants were recruited via Prolific and randomly assigned to one of three between-subjects conditions: a gain-framed message emphasising the benefits of conserving water, a loss-framed message emphasising the costs of failing to conserve, or a neutral informational control message. All participants were aged 18 or older, fluent in English, and residing in the United Kingdom. As specified in advance, participants who failed either of two embedded attention checks or who completed the study in under three minutes were excluded and replaced. The final sample comprised 310 participants: 95 in the gain-framed condition, 105 in the loss-framed condition, and 110 in the control condition. After reading their assigned message, participants completed the 8-item Water Conservation Attitude Scale (7-point Likert) and the 4-item behavioural intention index (7-point). Results. As planned, we conducted a one-way ANOVA on each outcome, followed by the two planned contrasts, with alpha = .05, two-tailed. Condition affected conservation attitudes, F(2, 307) = 6.42, p = .002; the planned contrast showed that the loss-framed condition produced more favourable attitudes than the control condition, t(307) = 3.21, p = .001, supporting H1. For behavioural intentions, the ANOVA was likewise significant, F(2, 307) = 4.88, p = .008, and the planned contrast showed that the gain-framed condition produced stronger intentions than the control condition, t(307) = 2.74, p = .006, supporting H2. No interim analyses were conducted.

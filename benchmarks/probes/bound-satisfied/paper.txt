Method. Participants. We recruited 310 participants via Prolific (United States residents, aged 18 or older, fluent in English). As specified in our preregistration, an a priori power analysis for a two-tailed independent-samples t-test (d = 0.33, alpha = .05, power = .80) indicated that 290 participants were required, and we committed to recruiting at least 300 to buffer against exclusions. All 310 participants passed the embedded attention check (an item instructing them to select "strongly agree"), so no participants were excluded, leaving 310 in the analysed sample. Procedure. Participants were randomly assigned to read either a morally framed appeal describing renewable energy adoption as an ethical obligation to future generations (n = 156) or an informationally equivalent neutral appeal presenting the same facts without moral language (n = 154). Measures. The primary outcome was the 12-item Renewable Energy Policy Support scale, rated on 7-point Likert scales and scored as the mean of the 12 items. We additionally recorded age, gender, and political orientation (a single 7-point item) for descriptive purposes only. Results. Consistent with H1, participants in the moral framing condition reported higher policy support (M = 5.12, SD = 1.08) than participants in the neutral framing condition (M = 4.83, SD = 1.14), Welch's t(306.4) = 2.30, p = .022, two-tailed, Cohen's d = 0.26, 95% CI [0.04, 0.48].

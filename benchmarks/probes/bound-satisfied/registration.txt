Preregistration: Moral Framing and Public Support for Renewable Energy Policy. Design: This is a between-subjects online experiment with two conditions. Participants will be randomly assigned to read either a morally framed appeal describing renewable energy adoption as an ethical obligation to future generations, or an informationally equivalent neutral appeal presenting the same facts without moral language. Sample: We will recruit at least 300 participants via Prolific (United States residents, aged 18 or older, fluent in English). An a priori power analysis for a two-tailed independent-samples t-test (d = 0.33, alpha = .05, power = .80) indicated that 290 participants are required; we commit to recruiting at least 300 to buffer against exclusions. Participants who fail the embedded attention check (an item instructing them to select "strongly agree") will be excluded prior to analysis. Measures: The primary outcome is the 12-item Renewable Energy Policy Support scale, rated on 7-point Likert scales and scored as the mean of the 12 items. We will additionally record age, gender, and political orientation (a single 7-point item) for descriptive purposes only. Hypothesis: H1: Participants in the moral framing condition will report higher policy support than participants in the neutral framing condition. Analysis plan: We will test H1 with a Welch's independent-samples t-test comparing condition means on policy support (alpha = .05, two-tailed), reporting Cohen's d with a 95% confidence interval.

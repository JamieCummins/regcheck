Study title: Retrieval practice and the retention of foreign-language vocabulary. Hypothesis: We predict that retrieval practice increases cued recall of vocabulary at a 48-hour delay relative to restudy. This is a confirmatory, directional prediction that we will test against the null hypothesis of no condition difference. Design: We will run a between-subjects experiment with two conditions, retrieval practice and restudy, with participants randomly assigned to condition. In the learning session, all participants first study the full set of materials; participants in the retrieval-practice condition then complete three cycles of cued-recall practice with feedback, whereas participants in the restudy condition re-read the materials for an equivalent amount of time. Sample: We will recruit 180 undergraduate students from the university participant pool, compensated with course credit. Participants who fail the embedded attention check, or who report prior participation in a study using these materials, will be excluded from analysis. Measures: Learning materials consist of 40 Swahili-English word pairs drawn from established norms. The dependent variable is the number of word pairs correctly recalled on a cued-recall test administered 48 hours after the learning session, scored as number correct out of 40. Analysis plan: We will test our prediction with an independent-samples t-test comparing the retrieval-practice and restudy conditions on 48-hour cued-recall performance (two-tailed, alpha = .05), and we will report Cohen's d as the effect size for this comparison.

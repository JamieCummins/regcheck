Method. We recruited 220 adult participants through Prolific. Following the preregistered criterion, 11 participants who responded "pleasant" on more than 95% or fewer than 5% of trials were excluded, leaving a final sample of 209. The experiment used a within-subjects design with one factor, prime type (plant-based product vs. conventional meat product). Implicit evaluations were assessed with the AMP. On each of 72 trials, a prime photograph (36 plant-based products, 36 conventional meat products) was presented for 75 ms, followed by a blank screen for 125 ms and a Chinese ideograph for 100 ms, which was then replaced by a black-and-white pattern mask. Participants judged whether the ideograph was more or less visually pleasant than the average ideograph. The primary dependent variable was the proportion of "pleasant" judgements following each prime type. Explicit attitudes were measured with three 7-point semantic-differential items (bad-good, unpleasant-pleasant, negative-positive) for each product category, averaged into a single index per category (Cronbach's alpha = .88 for plant-based, .91 for conventional meat). Results. As predicted (H1), participants made pleasant judgements on a higher proportion of trials following plant-based primes (M = .58, SD = .14) than conventional-meat primes (M = .51, SD = .13), two-tailed paired-samples t-test, t(208) = 5.42, p < .001, alpha = .05. Consistent with H2, the implicit difference score (plant-based minus conventional) correlated positively with the explicit difference score, r(207) = .29, p < .001.

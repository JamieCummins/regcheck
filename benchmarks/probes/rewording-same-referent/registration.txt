Study title: Implicit and explicit evaluations of plant-based meat alternatives. Hypotheses: H1: Participants will make pleasant judgements on a higher proportion of trials following plant-based primes than following conventional-meat primes. H2: The implicit difference score will correlate positively with the explicit difference score. Design: Within-subjects experiment with one factor, prime type (plant-based product vs. conventional meat product). Sample: We will recruit 220 adult participants through Prolific. Participants who respond "pleasant" on more than 95% or fewer than 5% of trials will be excluded from analysis. Measures: Our primary implicit measure is the Affect Misattribution Procedure, in which affective reactions to briefly presented prime images are misattributed to neutral targets. On each of 72 trials, a prime photograph (36 plant-based products, 36 conventional meat products) will be presented for 75 ms, followed by a blank screen for 125 ms and a Chinese ideograph for 100 ms, which is then replaced by a black-and-white pattern mask. Participants will judge whether the ideograph is more or less visually pleasant than the average ideograph. The primary dependent variable is the proportion of "pleasant" judgements following each prime type. Explicit attitudes will be measured with three 7-point semantic-differential items (bad-good, unpleasant-pleasant, negative-positive) for each product category, averaged into a single index per category. Analysis plan: H1 will be tested with a two-tailed paired-samples t-test (alpha = .05) comparing the proportion of pleasant responses following plant-based versus conventional-meat primes. H2 will be tested with a Pearson correlation between the implicit difference score (plant-based minus conventional) and the explicit difference score.

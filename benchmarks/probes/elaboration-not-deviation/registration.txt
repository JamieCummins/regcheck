Study title: Awe and the perception of available time. We will test whether inducing awe expands people's subjective sense of time availability relative to amusement and a neutral state. Design: between-subjects experiment with three conditions (awe, amusement, neutral). Participants: We will recruit 240 adult participants via Prolific (80 per condition), aged 18 to 65 and fluent in English. Participants who fail either of two embedded attention checks will be excluded and replaced, and data collection will stop once 240 participants who pass both attention checks have been obtained. Procedure: Participants will be randomly assigned to watch a 60-second video inducing awe (sweeping mountain vistas), amusement (comedy clips), or a neutral state (an instructional video about home appliances). They will then complete the Perceived Time Availability Scale (PTAS; 4 items rated on a 7-point Likert scale; alpha = .84 in prior work), which will be averaged into a single composite score. Hypothesis: We predict that emotion condition will influence perceived time availability, with the awe condition expected to yield the highest PTAS scores. Analysis plan: The primary confirmatory analysis will be a one-way between-subjects analysis of variance (ANOVA) on PTAS composite scores with condition (awe, amusement, neutral) as the between-subjects factor, using an alpha level of .05. No other analyses are planned.

Method. Following our preregistered plan, we recruited 240 adults via Prolific (80 per condition), aged 18 to 65 and fluent in English. Participants who failed either of the two embedded attention checks were excluded and replaced, yielding the target sample of 240 who passed both checks (M age = 34.2 years, SD = 11.6; 51% women). Participants were randomly assigned to watch a 60-second video inducing awe (sweeping mountain vistas), amusement (comedy clips), or a neutral state (an instructional video about home appliances), and then completed the Perceived Time Availability Scale (PTAS; 4 items rated on a 7-point Likert scale), which was averaged into a single composite score. Results. Descriptive statistics by condition were as follows: awe, M = 4.96 (SD = 1.03); amusement, M = 4.40 (SD = 1.10); neutral, M = 4.38 (SD = 1.07). Before conducting the preregistered analysis, we verified its assumptions: Levene's test indicated homogeneity of variances across conditions, F(2, 237) = 0.84, p = .43, and inspection of Q-Q plots indicated approximately normal residuals within each condition. The preregistered one-way between-subjects ANOVA on PTAS composite scores with condition (awe, amusement, neutral) as the between-subjects factor, tested at alpha = .05, revealed a significant effect of condition, F(2, 237) = 7.61, p < .001, partial eta squared = .060, 90% CI [.017, .110]. No analyses beyond the preregistered ANOVA were conducted.
